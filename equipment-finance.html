What Is Equipment Finance?

Equipment finance is a specialised form of business lending that allows Australian businesses to acquire the physical assets they need to operate and grow — without tying up working capital. Rather than paying the full purchase price upfront, you spread the cost over a term that matches the useful life of the asset, with the equipment itself typically serving as security.

Chattel Mortgage — Australia's Most Popular Equipment Finance

A chattel mortgage is the most widely used equipment finance product for Australian businesses because of its flexibility and tax advantages for GST-registered entities. Under a chattel mortgage:

  • You take ownership of the asset immediately upon purchase
  • The lender registers a security interest (mortgage) over the asset via the Personal Property Securities Register (PPSR)
  • If your business is GST-registered, you can claim the full GST input credit on the purchase price in your next BAS
  • You claim depreciation on the asset and interest on the loan as tax deductions
  • Optional balloon (residual) payment at the end can reduce monthly repayments
  • Terms typically 1–7 years

Finance Lease vs Operating Lease

For businesses that prefer off-balance-sheet treatment or want to upgrade equipment regularly, lease structures offer compelling advantages. Under a finance lease, you use the asset for the full term and have the option to purchase at the end for a residual value — 100% of lease payments are tax deductible, making this attractive for businesses with high taxable income. An operating lease is similar but the residual value is guaranteed by the lender; at the end of the term you simply return the asset or start a new lease, making it ideal for technology and equipment that depreciates rapidly.

Equipment Finance FAQ

Equipment finance is a type of asset-backed business lending that allows you to acquire physical assets — machinery, vehicles, technology, and more — by spreading the cost over a fixed term. The equipment typically serves as security, which helps keep rates competitive. Structures include chattel mortgage, hire purchase, finance lease and operating lease, each with different tax and ownership implications.

Almost any business-use equipment can be financed. Common categories include: construction and earthmoving machinery, agricultural equipment, medical and dental equipment, manufacturing plant and machinery, IT and technology hardware, hospitality equipment, transport vehicles, forklifts, printing equipment, and much more. A balloon payment (also called a residual value) is a lump sum payment at the end of a chattel mortgage or hire purchase agreement. By including a balloon, your monthly repayments are reduced — but you must pay the lump sum at the end of the term. You can pay it in cash, refinance it, or sell/trade the asset. Balloons are optional on chattel mortgages and typically range from 10%–30% of the original loan amount. They are required on finance leases.
