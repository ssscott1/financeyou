Commercial finance encompasses the full spectrum of lending products designed to help businesses start, grow, and thrive. Unlike consumer lending, commercial finance focuses on the needs of businesses — from sole traders and SMEs to large corporations. Business Loans — What You Need to Know

A business term loan provides a lump sum that you repay with interest over an agreed period. They can be:

  • Secured: backed by assets such as property, equipment or a personal guarantee — generally lower rates
  • Unsecured: no collateral required — faster approval, higher rates, typically lower maximums
  • Fixed rate: certainty of repayment — ideal for budgeting
  • Variable rate: can benefit from rate drops — flexibility to make extra repayments

Loan amounts for secured business lending can range from $50,000 to several million dollars depending on the security and business financials. Finance You works with both traditional bank lenders and non-bank specialists who can move faster and consider more complex situations.

Invoice Finance — Unlock Your Working Capital

Many Australian businesses suffer cash flow problems not because they lack revenue, but because customers take 30, 60 or even 90 days to pay. Invoice finance solves this by allowing you to access up to 90% of the value of outstanding invoices within 24 hours of issuing them. When your customer pays, the remaining balance (less the lender's fee) is released to you.

There are two main types:

  • Invoice factoring: the finance company manages debtor collection on your behalf
  • Invoice discounting: you retain control of debtor management — confidential facility

Commercial Finance FAQ

Commercial finance is a broad category of business lending that includes term loans, lines of credit, invoice finance, trade finance, asset finance and commercial property loans. Unlike consumer finance, commercial lending is primarily regulated by the Banking Act and is not subject to the National Consumer Credit Protection Act (except where personal guarantees are involved for credit-regulated products).

Requirements vary by lender and loan type. Generally, lenders want to see: an active ABN (2+ years preferred), evidence of business revenue, recent financial statements (BAS or tax returns), bank statements (3–12 months), and details of any existing debts. For secured loans, details of the proposed security (property, equipment) are also required. Some lenders have more flexible criteria — contact Finance You to discuss your situation.

Business loan amounts range from $5,000 (small unsecured loans) to $5 million+ (secured commercial property and large asset finance). The maximum amount depends on your business revenue, assets, credit history, and the type of security offered. For unsecured business loans, most lenders cap amounts at $250,000–$500,000 without additional security.

Invoice finance allows businesses to advance up to 90% of the value of outstanding invoices, rather than waiting 30–90 days for customers to pay. It's ideal for B2B businesses with significant outstanding debtor books — typically $50,000+ in debtors. The cost is typically 1%–3% of the invoice value per month, which is often far less than the cost of lost sales or late payment penalties from your own suppliers.

Business loan rates vary widely depending on the loan type, security, term, and borrower profile. Secured business loans with strong financials can start from around 7.5%–9% p.a. Unsecured business loans generally range from 10%–25% p.a. Invoice finance and short-term working capital products are typically priced as a factor rate or monthly fee rather than a traditional interest rate. Finance You searches 40+ lenders to find the most competitive rate for your situation.

Trade finance facilitates domestic and international trade by providing payment guarantees or short-term lending to bridge the gap between when you pay your suppliers and when you receive payment from your customers. Products include letters of credit, import finance, export finance, supply chain finance, and trade credit insurance. Finance You can connect you with specialist trade finance lenders.

Many lenders require personal guarantees from directors or major shareholders for business loans, particularly for small businesses and unsecured lending. A personal guarantee means you personally take responsibility for repaying the loan if your business cannot. Understanding the implications of personal guarantees is important — Finance You's brokers will explain all requirements clearly before you commit to any finance arrangement.
