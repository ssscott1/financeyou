FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about working with Finance You.

A finance broker acts as an intermediary between borrowers and lenders. Rather than applying directly to one bank and accepting whatever rate they offer, Finance You approaches our panel of 40+ lenders simultaneously, comparing products and negotiating on your behalf to find the most suitable loan for your circumstances. We handle all the paperwork and communication with lenders, saving you time and increasing your chances of approval.

Our broking service is generally free to you. We are remunerated through commissions paid by lenders when your loan settles, typically 0.5%–1.5% of the loan amount. We disclose all commissions and fees transparently before you commit to any finance arrangement, as required under our Australian Credit Licence obligations. Some complex commercial transactions may involve an upfront broker fee, which will always be disclosed upfront.

For straightforward equipment, car and truck loans, conditional approval can often be obtained within 2–4 hours of submitting a complete application. Formal approval and settlement typically takes 24–48 hours. More complex commercial transactions or applications requiring additional documentation may take longer. We'll always give you a realistic timeline upfront.

Requirements vary by loan type and lender. For most business equipment and vehicle loans, you'll typically need: photo ID, ABN/ACN, 6 months bank statements, and a quote or invoice for the asset. For consumer loans, ID and recent payslips or bank statements are usually sufficient. We offer low-documentation options for eligible borrowers — ask your broker for details.

A full credit enquiry is recorded when a formal application is lodged with a lender. However, Finance You can often perform a soft assessment of your situation before making a formal application, which does not affect your credit file. We work hard to match you with the right lender first time to minimise unnecessary credit enquiries.

Yes — our panel includes specialist lenders who work with customers who have imperfect credit histories, defaults, or previous bankruptcies. The interest rate and loan terms may differ from prime lending, and we'll always ensure any loan is suitable for your circumstances under our responsible lending obligations. We encourage you to contact us to discuss your situation confidentially.

A chattel mortgage is a loan where you own the asset from day one, and the lender takes a mortgage (security interest) over it until the loan is repaid. You can claim GST input credits upfront, depreciation, and interest as tax deductions. A finance lease means the lender owns the asset and you lease it, paying monthly rent. At the end, you can purchase the asset for a residual value. Lease payments are generally 100% tax deductible. The right structure depends on your business's tax position and cash flow needs — our brokers can advise.
